# DIA-022 — Hit versus error: delete `ordinaryEffort`, charge from consequences (§13.2, §4.2)
**Spec:** §13.2 (hit-vs-error, the misplay set), §13.5 (the override pattern), §1 #4 (physics, not
rulings), §14 (seven acceptance plays carry the field)
**Sequencing note:** **M1**, and it gates DIA-009 the way DIA-015 does: a play that enters cleanly
and derives wrongly is an M1 blocker under "99.9% of possible plays score correctly."
**Goal:** remove the `ordinaryEffort` flag, derive error charging from what the misplay actually
cost, and ask the batter's credit as *how far she earned* rather than as a boolean about the fielder.

## Why

Mark, 2026-09-02: *"ordinary effort sounded good to me, because as a coach, you often are saying 'is
that a play we normally should make?' But that's a different question than error/hit, so I think we
need to get rid of ordinary effort and just let scorers make hit/error judgments."*

He is right, and the reason makes this change **more** consistent with the architecture rather than
less. Diamond records physics and derives rulings. `ordinaryEffort` is **neither**: nobody observes
ordinary effort, so it is not physics; and it is not the ruling either. It is a *proxy* for the
ruling, so the scorer translates the judgment she actually holds ("that's a hit") into a different
question ("would she normally make that play?") for the app to translate back. A judgment dressed as
an observation is the one thing §13 did not intend to have.

**It is also redundant with the touch vocabulary.** `DROPPED` means she had it and lost it;
`MISSED_CATCH` means she never handled it. *You cannot drop what you could not reach.* So "catchable"
in Play #1's development view — *"SS charged a dropped catchable liner"* — is not an extra judgment,
it is a restatement of what `DROPPED` already asserts. The misplay types stay; Mark: *"we still need
to know that she dropped a liner and should still be entering it."*

**And one boolean structurally cannot say what scorers say.** A clean single followed by a boot that
lets her take second is *a hit and an error*. `ordinaryEffort` has no way to express it, which is why
the boot currently swallows the single (below).

## The bug this fixes, verified

`play_draft.dart`'s `recordingFielderPlay`: a misplay on the **first touch** claims the batter's
reach unconditionally.

```
BEFORE boot -> reach enabledByKey: null
AFTER  boot -> reach enabledByKey: 1     # the boot now explains her reaching first
```

So a fly ball that lands untouched for a clean single, then is booted, derives as **reached on an
error with the hit destroyed**. And it cannot be corrected: the chain strip's leg sheet offers only
*"Re-attribute — tap the enabling touch"*, so the link can be re-pointed at another touch but never
cleared, and there is only one touch on the play.

The charge is not the problem — the boot **should** be charged. Only the *link* is misattributed.
`official_scoring.dart` already says the right thing (*"an uncharged misplay means the reach was a
hit"*), so turning ordinary effort off does restore the single — and uncharges the error with it,
losing the E7. Charge and link are separate facts and only one of them is wrong.

**Also:** the claim reorders the chain wrongly. After the boot, the strip shows `LEG 1->2` before
`LEG 0->1` — the extra base ahead of the reach that preceded it.

## The model

**An error is charged when a misplay gave a base that was not earned** — rule 9.12 read literally:
prolonging an at-bat or the life of a runner. So:

| play | earned | given | result |
|---|---|---|---|
| boot on a routine grounder, she reaches | nothing | first | **E** |
| clean single, boot lets her take second | first | second | **hit + E** |
| boot, but she recovers and throws her out | — | nothing | **no error** |

That last row is the tell. `ordinaryEffort` gets it right only by accident — the misplay *is*
ordinary effort, and no error is charged because nothing followed. Deriving from consequences makes
that the rule instead of a coincidence.

**A misplay is still required.** Mark: *"we need some kind of misplay on a batted ball to even bring
up an error. We're either going to have a fielder booting or missing it or we're going to have a bad
throw."* No misplay, no error. Touchless errors — failure to cover, failure to cut off — are **not**
M1; §13.2 already special-cases the one that mattered (catcher's interference, "E2 by rule").

**The question the surface asks becomes "how far did she earn?"** — which the data model already
supports, because a multi-base advance is stored as separate legs, each independently attributable:

| answer | 0→1 | 1→2 |
|---|---|---|
| Single, 2nd on the error | unattributed | → the misplay |
| Double | unattributed | unattributed |
| Two-base error | → the misplay | → the misplay |

Mark's own three options. It degenerates correctly at first base — earned one is a single, earned
none is a reach on error — so the first-base case is the same question, not a special case.

## Slices

**022a — the fixture diff, first and before anything changes.** Seven of the fifteen acceptance plays
carry `ordinaryEffort`. Derive them **without** it, charging from consequences, and diff the outputs.
Expectation: they agree — Play #1 is "no error, no consequence" and consequence-derivation gives
exactly that; Play #2's boot and wild throw both produce unearned bases and both still charge. **If
all seven agree, the flag was never carrying information and the reform is safe by demonstration.**
Any play that disagrees is precisely where the judgment mattered, and it defines what the new model
must express. Do not skip this and do not mechanically rewrite the fixtures — they are the definition
of correct.

**022b — stop the auto-claim.** A first-touch misplay no longer claims the reach; the reorder goes
with it.

**022c — ask how far she earned.** On the advance, only when a misplay makes it ambiguous. Asking on
every advance is more consistent and far more taps; start narrow.

**022d — delete the flag.** Schema (§21.5 protocol: minimal edit, regenerate both targets, one atomic
commit), `defaultOrdinaryEffort`, the derivation gate, the chain strip's switch, §13.2's prose.

## Open

- **The development view loses "catchable" as a separate assertion.** The argument above is that
  `DROPPED` already carries it. Worth confirming against §17's development outputs before deleting,
  because Play #1's whole point is that record.
- **Marking a mistake that cost nothing.** Under this model a misplay with no unearned base charges
  nothing, which is correct by rule but may not match what a scorer expects to be able to say. Mark,
  2026-09-02: post-M1, scorers may *"notate a play in a way that identifies a mistake, but not an
  error."* Parked there deliberately.
- **An error override.** §13.5 gives earned runs and RBIs an override and gives the error none —
  odd, since rule 9.12 is the most explicit judgment the book hands the scorer. Not required by this
  ticket, which makes the derivation match the judgment; worth revisiting if it still disagrees.

## Accept

The seven fixtures derive identically without the flag, or every difference is understood and
recorded; a clean single followed by a boot scores as a hit **and** an error; a misplay that costs
nothing charges nothing; `ordinaryEffort` appears nowhere in `schema/`, `app/lib`, or §13.2.
