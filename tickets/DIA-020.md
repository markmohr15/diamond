# DIA-020 — Editing history: correct, insert, delete (§6, §11.3, §12.5)
**Spec:** §6 (corrections), §2 (`effectiveAfter`), §11.2 (base state is never guessed), §11.3,
§12.3 (pitch summary), §12.5 (CountCorrection), §19 (event inspector)
**Sequencing note:** **post-M1.** Nothing here makes a play score wrongly today; it makes a *mistake*
expensive to fix. DIA-019f is the M1 half — it stops undo at the plate appearance, which is the door
this ticket eventually opens.
**Goal:** give the scorer a way to change what already happened that is not the undo button — correct
a recorded outcome, insert an event that was missed, delete one that never happened — without
guessing at anything the stream does not know.

## Why: one root cause, three symptoms

Mark, 2026-08-31: *"When you call a pitch and hit the checkmark, that's a real thing that happened
that you wouldn't think would need to be undone ever."*

That is the whole ticket. `PitchThrown` bundles two things that differ in kind:

- **the pitch happened** — called this, thrown, located there. Once the checkmark is hit, this is a
  fact about the world. It is never wrong.
- **the outcome** — ball, strike, foul. An entry, and the only part that can be mistapped.

Undo voids the composite, so it asserts *the pitch was never thrown*, which is false. Three separate
problems trace to the same place — the call and the checkmark have **no stream presence** until the
outcome commits:

1. **Undo lies.** Fixing a mistapped outcome voids a true fact and then quietly re-records it.
2. **A crash loses a real pitch.** Between the checkmark and the outcome tap a pitch has been thrown
   and nothing is in the stream. The play draft is journaled against this (§15.5); the pitch flow is
   not.
3. **DIA-019f's seal cannot be derived.** "She started calling the next pitch" leaves no trace, so
   the seal is UI state with a restart fallback rather than a fact about the game.

**The open modeling question this ticket should decide first:** does the checkmark get its own event?
It would fix all three at the root. It is also a real change — a second event per pitch, sync
implications (§12), and every projection that assumes one `PitchThrown` per pitch. Not obviously
worth it; worth *deciding* rather than drifting.

## The three operations, which are not equally hard

| | what it means | state today |
|---|---|---|
| **correct** | this happened, we wrote the wrong result | §6 exists and works; needs a surface |
| **insert** | this happened and we missed it | machinery **built end to end, no writer** |
| **delete** | this never happened | `VoidEvent` exists; one-way, see below |

**Correct** is the common case and the cheapest. A mistapped outcome is a §6 correction: the pitch
stays at its position, `corrects` points at the original, provenance shows when the fix arrived. One
event instead of undo's void-plus-re-record, and it says what actually happened. What it needs is
somewhere to *see* the wrong pitch — §12.3's pitch summary and §19's event inspector are both already
specified as places where a pitch is visible and editable. This may be the first thing that forces
one of them to be real.

**Insert** is `effectiveAfter`, which is **already built and has never been written**:
`spliceEffectiveAfter` repositions a tagged event to sit immediately after its anchor,
`game_state_projector` guards snapshots against inserts at or before the boundary (with tests,
including the backdated insert), and `event_store` persists the column. `GameController.append` does
not even take the parameter. There is a working feature here with no way to trigger it.

**Delete** is `VoidEvent` and is **one-way**: `applyVisibility` collects voided roots and hides them,
and voids are skipped before they are ever resolved, so voiding a void does nothing. Un-delete is not
available without a model change.

**Redo is assessed and rejected.** Every voided event survives in the raw stream with its call and
location intact, so the *data* for redo is already permanent — but resurrection is impossible
(above), so redo would mean re-appending the payload as a new event: three events for a round trip
the scorer thinks of as zero. More to the point, redo is a workaround for undo being the only
correction tool. Ask why a scorer peels back four pitches and it is almost always to fix something in
the first one; with **correct** available she never peels back at all, and there is nothing to redo.

## Insert: what re-derives, what remaps, what gets flagged

The design question Mark posed: *"maybe you keep a play but you change the players involved because
you missed some girl getting HBP so you insert the HBP. Would we be able to account for that and just
ask if the baserunners and batter should be adjusted for everything that came after?"*

The answer splits along the stored/derived line, and the good news is that the two failures have
**different blast radii**.

**Re-derives for free.** Count, outs, score, base occupancy, RBI, earned runs — all projections, so
the fold re-runs. The batting *order position* too: `batterDue` reads the lineup and a completed-PA
count and is "never cached positionally (spec §4.4 design constraint)", so an inserted HBP advances
the order on its own.

**Remaps mechanically — offer it.** `PitchThrown.batterId` is *stored*, computed from the projection
at write time and then frozen. So after an insert the derived order says batter 6 is due while every
recorded event says batter 5 took that PA. The fix is deterministic: shift every stored `batterId`
after the insert one position in the order, as a §6 correction per event. This is what Mark's "just
ask" can actually cover, and it lasts the rest of the game.

**Cannot be adjusted by consent — flag it.** The inserted HBP puts an **extra runner on base**, so
every later play in that half-inning happened in a base state nobody recorded. A recorded tag out at
second may have been a force. A single that scored one may have scored two. Whether that runner
advanced on each later play is information **the stream does not contain**. That is re-adjudication,
not consent, and guessing it is barred outright: §11.2's non-negotiable is that base state is never
wrong and never guessed — uncertainty is surfaced and resolved at a checkpoint.

**So: accept the insert, offer the batter remap, and flag the plays from the insert to the end of
that half-inning for review.** The scorer walks the flagged plays. Same move the count HUD already
makes when it goes amber: surface the uncertainty, refuse to guess.

**The half-inning bound is what makes this affordable.** Base-state damage ends when the bases clear,
so the hard problem is capped at one half-inning while the unbounded problem (the order shift) is the
mechanical one. And a missed HBP is normally noticed in the same half-inning, so the flagged region
is usually one or two plays. **The feature is useful long before the general case is solved.**

## Suggested order

1. **Decide the checkmark question** — its own event, or not. Everything else is easier to place once
   this is settled.
2. **Correct**, on the simplest surface that shows a pitch. The common case, the cheapest, and the
   one that removes most of the demand for the others.
3. **Insert, non-count-changing first** — a missed stolen base. It moves no count and ends no plate
   appearance, so it exercises `effectiveAfter`'s first writer without needing the remap or the flag.
4. **Insert, count-changing** — the HBP case, with the remap and the half-inning flag.
5. **Delete**, last, and only if it is still wanted by then.
