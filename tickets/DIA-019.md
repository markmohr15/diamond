# DIA-019 — Scoring entry: defects and the UI/UX that makes it usable (§11.1, §11.3, §15)
**Spec:** §11.1 (the loop, and v0.39's location backfill), §11.3 (consequences, D3K), §15.1–§15.3
(field surface and chain strip), §23.1.4 (dark mode)
**Sequencing note:** M1, and it runs **before** DIA-009. DIA-009's accept is a manual script Mark
performs on a tablet and signs off; that script has to execute against a fixed app, or the run that
passed is not the app that ships.
**Goal:** close the enumerated set of known defects and rough edges in the scoring entry path, so
M1's "the known small bugs are **gone**, not triaged" is checkable against a list rather than
against memory.

**Scope widened 2026-09-02 (Mark):** *"We're going to do all sorts of things that improve UI/UX
here."* So this is no longer only defects. The list below is still enumerated and still closed at any
moment — additions are deliberate, which is the property worth keeping — but it now carries
improvements as well as bugs, and slices H onward come from Mark's own simulator pass rather than
from a code audit.

## Slices

### A — Location backfill after a dropped third strike (§11.1, §11.3)

Approved 2026-08-30. §11.1 v0.39's "record last pitch" offer already exists in full
(`RecordLastPitchOffer`, `PitchStep.recordLast`, backfill as a §6 correction). It is armed only for
`outcome == IN_PLAY`, which is narrower than the spec's own principle: §11.1 says a pitch missing its
location can be given one at any time, and the *rationale* is that on contact the scorer's attention
leaves the zone. A D3K is that same situation — the batter is running.

1. Arm `lastPitchOffer` on `IN_PLAY || d3k != null`, both location fields still null
   (`pitch_flow.dart`, in `commitOutcome`).
2. **The bounce hinge on the recordLast canvas.** Mark: "have to have bounce available there." A
   D3K's signature case is a pitch in the dirt, so without it the slice half-works on the case that
   motivates it. `onCommitBounce` is already an optional `ZoneCanvas` param; add a
   `recordLastBounce` writing `bounceLocation` into the same §6 correction. Backfill becomes the
   same canvas as live entry — only what the release writes differs.
3. `pitch_loop_page.dart`'s "No bounce hinge: the offer exists only for in-play pitches" goes stale.
4. Tests: the offer stands after each of the four D3K endings and after the `d3kToField` round trip;
   taking it lands the correction; the bounce path writes `bounceLocation` and not `actualLocation`.
5. Spec bump, three places.

Nearly free, because the four D3K resolutions and `d3kToField` already reset with
`PitchFlowState(lastPitchOffer: state.lastPitchOffer)` — a standing offer survives them with no
plumbing.

**Decided:** do *not* arm it for every unlocated pitch. A scorer who routinely skips location would
get the affordance on nearly every call screen; in-play and D3K share a justification that takes and
balls do not. And it arms at commit, so it stands regardless of how the D3K ended — out on the throw
included.

### B — Undo should not discard the call and the location (§11.3)

Mark: *"if we record a pitch, say a ball, and then click undo, we're undoing the location and the
call as well. This is wrong."*

The call, the actual location, and the outcome are held in `PitchFlowState` and written as one
`PitchThrown`. `undoLast` voids that event, which is right for the *stream* — but it also throws away
two pieces of entry the scorer never wanted to redo. Mistap "Ball" for "Called strike" and you
re-enter the wristband code and the zone to fix a wrong button.

The data needed is in the voided payload. Undo of a just-committed pitch should void it, repopulate
`PitchFlowState` from that payload (`intendedType`, `intendedZoneId`, `intendedLocation`,
`actualLocation`, `bounceLocation`), and return to the **outcome step** so the scorer re-answers only
the question they got wrong.

**Decided (Mark, 2026-08-30):** consequences the loop wrote itself — the automatic strikeout out, a
walk's forced chain — stay voided. *"If it happened automatically, then one undo should undo
everything that happened automatically."* So the undo unit is unchanged; what changes is only that
the scorer's own entry is handed back to her instead of thrown away. Nothing she typed is
automatic, and nothing automatic survives.

### C — Step undo on the field draft (§15.2) — **done, inside B's PR**

Parked on 2026-08-30 for simulator time, and the simulator time answered it: the two-undos confusion
Mark hit **is** this slice's problem. So it shipped with the undo unification rather than separately
— the top bar's undo needs something to delegate to on the field, and this is that something.

**It was cheaper than the sizing note below assumed.** `PlayDraft` is immutable and every mutation
already funnels through one method, so a stack of previous drafts is the entire mechanism, with no
inverse operation per action. The stack is session state, not journaled: a relaunch restores the
draft (§15.5) but not its history, which is right — the crash journal exists so a half-entered play
survives, not so it can be rewound afterwards.

The description below is kept as written, but two things in it are now out of date: `reset()` is
**deleted** (starting a play over is what `✕` does, in place), and step undo replaced it rather
than joining it.

The same mismatch as B, on the other surface: "undo the last action" and "back up one step" are
different operations. `reset()` (the unlabeled ↺) throws the whole draft away and `removeEntry(key)`
removes one node the scorer can point at, but there is no "take back what I just did" — and what she
just did is often a *batch* (a throw writes a touch and a leg together), which is exactly what a
per-node removal cannot express.

Snapshot-based: push the draft before each mutating action, pop on step undo. Sizing this is part of
the slice.

### D — A way to choose dark mode (§23.1.4)

Both themes are built and tested, and nothing selects between them: there is no `ThemeMode` anywhere
in `lib/` and no settings surface. The app follows the system, which is the wrong default for the
use — a tablet in a dugout at a 9pm game is not reliably in system dark, and the scorer cannot fix it
from inside the app.

Smallest honest version: a persisted three-state preference (system / light / dark) and somewhere to
tap it. No new design work; the themes exist.

### E — "Fielded" and "Picked it up" (§15.2) — **shipped, and incomplete: see H/I**

**Superseded in part by slice I.** This slice relabeled the `!battedBall` branch and left the
airborne one, on the reading below that a dropped fly is "picked up." Mark reversed that on
2026-09-02 once the inconsistency showed up in the simulator: a batted ball is *fielded* however it
is entered. What survives here is the `!battedBall` relabel, which was right.

**Decided (Mark, 2026-08-30):** both words survive, classified together. *"After you drop a fly
ball, you don't really field it, you do pick it up. However, does that matter for our purposes?
Either way, you take possession of it."* It does not matter: the distinction is linguistic, not
physical, and §13 charges on the physical fact.

That is **already what the code does** — the labels are context-gated and both write
`TouchType.FIELDED`, so nothing downstream can tell them apart:

| branch | label |
|---|---|
| airborne, in flight | `Picked it up` |
| ground ball | `Fielded` |
| `!battedBall` (between pitches) | `Fielded` |

**The one thing to fix:** the third row. A catcher chasing a wild pitch to the backstop is the same
"ball on the ground, take possession" case as the dropped fly, so by the rule above it should read
`Picked it up`. The comment immediately above that entry already says "she picked it up" while the
label says otherwise. A one-line relabel, no type change.

### F — Undo stops at the plate appearance (§6, §11.3)

Decided 2026-08-31, out of the conversation slice B started. B fixed *what undo throws away*; this
fixes *how far back it reaches*, and the two are the same button.

**The rule:**

> A plate appearance **seals** when the next one begins, and stays sealed. Undo reaches anything
> unsealed.

"Begins" is **calling the next pitch** — the first type tap — or, when the call is skipped, the
first action belonging to that pitch. That signal is not invented here: the record-last-pitch offer
already dies on exactly it, and says so at `pitch_loop_page.dart:118`: *"the loop moving on is
itself the dismissal, and starting the next call IS moving on: the first type tap removes it."*
This generalizes that to undo.

Sealing is a **high-water mark**, and that is the load-bearing part. An earlier draft of the rule
put the floor at "the start of the plate appearance containing the most recent action," which
bounds nothing: peel a PA empty and the most recent action moves into the previous one, taking the
floor with it, all the way to the first pitch of the game. Monotonic sealing does not move
backwards — undo everything in PA 6 and PA 5 stays sealed, because PA 6 having started is a fact
about history, not about the current state.

**Why it is worth doing.** Every hazard around editing history — shifted counts, a PA that ended
earlier than recorded, later pitches belonging to a different batter — needs undo to cross a PA
boundary. Confine it inside one and none of them are reachable by the everyday button. Undo stops
meaning "rewind the game" and means "take back what I just did," which is what a scorer means by it.
Going further back is DIA-020's job, deliberately through a different surface.

**It contradicts the spec, so it carries a bump.** §6 ("Instant, unlimited depth") and §11.3
("Undo is always one tap, top-level, unlimited depth") both promise the opposite. This is a revision,
not a defect fix.

**It also revises a passing test.** `pitch_loop_acceptance_test` peels three levels *across* PA
boundaries on purpose — undo #2 is commented "opp-2's PA reopens" and undo #3 takes the previous
batter's walk. Under the seal rule undo #3 is refused. DIA-009's manual script inherits that same
sequence, which is the concrete reason this lands **before** DIA-009 rather than after.

**The seal cannot be derived from the stream.** The call lives in `callDraftProvider` and nothing is
written until the outcome commits, so "she started calling the next pitch" leaves no trace. Live,
`callDraftProvider.pitchTypeId != null` is the signal. After a relaunch the draft is empty and the
app cannot know, so **a restart seals**: undo reaches only into the current incomplete PA. Closing
the app right after a strikeout costs the ability to undo it, which errs toward protecting history,
and a restart is itself a strong "moved on."

**No prerequisite, and an earlier draft of this ticket was wrong to claim one.** It said the floor
needed plate-appearance boundaries lifted out of `official_scoring.dart`, sharing work with DIA-009's
box score. It does not. The boundary is a `batterId` change on `PitchThrown` —
`official_scoring.dart:399` detects it with nothing more than that — and `undoLast` already walks the
visible stream backwards, so it stops on the same change in a few lines. DIA-009 needs plate
appearance and at-bat *totals*, which is a different need; "both involve plate appearances" was
mistaken for "both need the same code."

**Ships inside slice B's PR, not its own.** F's refusal needs the undo button to *show* it is
unavailable, and B rewired that call site — split apart, one PR makes the button do something new
and the other makes it sometimes decline, with the disabled state landing awkwardly in neither. They
are also the same function and would collide. Kept as separate sections here because the reasoning
behind each is worth keeping distinct.

### H — the menus offer only what is possible (§4.3, §15.1, §15.3)

One bug written four times. An option is gated on a condition that is *necessary but not sufficient*,
so it is offered in states where it cannot be true. Tag-on-a-caught-fly was this and is fixed; these
are the rest.

- **Fielder's choice with nobody to choose against.** The gate is `if (!caught)` alone. A fielder's
  choice means the defense elected to retire someone else — it needs another runner on base, or an
  out recorded on someone else this play. Today it is offered on a bases-empty fly ball.
- **Deflection in the outfield.** Offered for a line drive still in flight, and unconditionally once
  the ball is down. Mark: *"I don't think Deflected ever makes sense in the outfield. You either
  missed it, you booted it, you caught it, or you fielded it."* Deflection earns its place in the
  infield because it is mechanically load-bearing — `wasInFlight` treats it as leaving the ball
  airborne, so a liner off the pitcher's glove can still be caught. Nothing equivalent happens 250
  feet out.
- **The chain strip's retype menu is ungated entirely.** It offers all seven misplay types on every
  node, while the entry popup beside it is carefully branched three ways. So the popup refuses to
  offer "Booted" to a catcher chasing a wild pitch and the chain strip offers it anyway. Found on a
  pickoff, where the receiving node offered both `Wild throw` and `The throw was wild` — two controls
  with near-identical wording and different meanings.
- **Look-back, and leaving early.** Rules at *every level* (Mark, 2026-09-03), so no `RuleSet` gates
  them and DIA-017 no longer lists them. Whether a crew calls them varies by level and by crew, and
  the app cannot know which umpire is working — so the option exists everywhere and the scorer
  records what was called. Leaving early is *sometimes done deliberately as a mercy* to the other
  team, so the entry must not assume it is a defensive event.
- **Infield fly, which is a *suppressor* rather than a capability.** Mark: *"there is no force when
  an infield fly happens and that would be valuable to not display in that case… if they choose it,
  we can eliminate offering force out or fielders choice."* §15.1 already has the identical rule for
  a catch — "a catch removes every force" — and an infield fly does the same thing whether or not it
  is caught, which is the part the code cannot currently express. Keep it visible because a scorer
  will look for it; the value is what it turns *off*.

**The fix is one mechanism, not four patches.** Three surfaces each hand-maintain a list of what is
possible, and they disagree. A menu should offer only what this touch, this runner, this base state
could have been, derived in one place.

**Throw vocabulary, decided (Mark, 2026-09-02):** a throw arriving is *bad*, *caught*, *missed*, or
*missed tag*. Nothing else. Those map onto existing types with no schema change — `WILD_THROW` on the
**thrower's** node (which is what "The throw was wild" already does), `RECEIVED_THROW`,
`MISSED_CATCH`, `TAG_MISSED` — and removing `WILD_THROW` from the receiving node's chip set dissolves
the duplicate. `_receivingTypes` then loses `DROPPED`, so the arrival-quality chips gate on
`RECEIVED_THROW` + `MISSED_CATCH`, which still reads right.

**Infield is 1–6, outfield is 7 and up**, the tenth fielder included. Mark wants `OF4` available
later and always treated as an outfielder, so this is a named predicate rather than a repeated
`position < 7`. The deflection gate is its first caller; infield fly is its second.

### I — one word per thing (§15.1)

`Fielded` and `Picked it up` are the same `TouchType.FIELDED` behind two labels, and which one you
get depends on **how you entered the play**, not what happened:

- fly ball, **one tap**, drag the fielder to where it ends up → `Picked it up`
- fly ball, **two taps**, drag the fielder to the ball → `Fielded`

Same physical play. Tracing the roll flips `inFlight`, which selects a different branch, which
changes the verb — and nothing on screen explains why. Path-dependent vocabulary makes the surface
look arbitrary, which is worse than either word being wrong.

**The rule (Mark):** *"Fielded should be our verbage in both cases."* A **batted ball is fielded**; a
**pitch that got away is picked up**. Airborne has nothing to do with it, which also frees `inFlight`
to keep only the job it should have — deciding whether *Caught* is still on offer.

Slice E fixed the `!battedBall` instance and left the airborne one, so two of three branches now say
"Picked it up" and the odd one out is a batted ball. The comment there asserting a dropped fly is
"the same case as" a catcher at the backstop is the equivalence being separated — delete it, do not
edit it.

### J — the runner's arrival is where her questions get asked (§11.3, §13.6, §15.1)

Three findings that collapse into one change.

**A cascaded run is asserted, never confirmed.** With runners on second and third and the batter
dragged to second, the cascade pushes R2→3 and R3→**home**, and a leg to 4 *is* a scoring advance. So
a run is recorded that nobody confirmed. The chain is sound — R2 cannot stay on second once the
batter is there, so R3 has to leave third — but *"she left third"* and *"she scored"* are different
claims and only the first follows. She can be thrown out at the plate, which on a bases-loaded single
is a real play.

**And she is invisible while it happens.** `field_painter.dart` draws a scored runner at **25% alpha
against 75% for everyone else**. The intent was de-emphasis — the cascade's comment says a scored
leader is "out of the way" — but against §18.7's sunlight-glanceable bar that token does not exist.
Invisible *and* unconfirmed is why this went unnoticed.

**So ask at the push, not at commit.** GameChanger asks "did the runner score?" and asking at the
moment of the play is right; a commit-time gate would be the disabled-button failure mode again — a
control that declines without saying which of six things is unfinished. Only the runner pushed to
home is asked; the one pushed to third is in a resting state.

**The sacrifice judgment moves onto the same question.** Today it is a lone `FilterChip` floating at
the bottom of the surface, and its label is the constant `'Sacrifice bunt'` — so after DIA-015
widened `invitesSacrifice` to cover clause (2), a **dropped fly** with a runner scoring offers you
"Sacrifice bunt" on a ball nobody bunted. The derivation knows better; only the label lies. Mark:
*"can it be a function of the runner… we move the runner home on a ball in the air and then we have a
checkbox if it was a sac fly."*

That is also what §15.3 already decided for the ⚖ calls — *"baserunning facts, so they live on the
runner-consequence sheets — never on a standing button."* The sacrifice chip is the standing button
that rule was written against.

**Keep two things as they are:** `sacrifice` stays stored on the `BallInPlay`, because the credit is
the *batter's* — only the question moves, so this needs no schema change. And clause (1), a ball
caught with a runner scoring, keeps deriving silently; `_sacrificeFor` is explicit that it must not
start asking there.

**Accepted cost:** one tap on the most common scoring play there is. It is the trade DIA-014c already
made ("one tap, not zero"), and GameChanger's users pay it too.

### K — stolen base beside wild pitch and passed ball (§13.2, §15.6)

Mark: *"we could put stolen base with Wild pitch and passed ball and do the same runner up one
thing."* A steal currently costs a trip to §15.6's idle field for what is usually one gesture.

The chips are already single-select through one field, so a steal gets **mutual exclusion for free** —
which is exactly right, because a runner going on a ball that gets away is a judgment between "she
would have made it anyway" (steal) and "the ball moved her" (wild pitch). Forcing one answer is
correct. The events stay clean too: WP/PB set `cause`, a steal is `reason: STOLEN_BASE` with no
cause, which is the split v0.50 introduced.

**Two things it is not.** "All runners up one" is right for a loose ball and wrong for a steal —
first and third, the runner on third almost always holds. The existing prompt already offers *go to
the field* as the other door, so a multi-runner steal takes it and nothing new is needed. And **caught
stealing still needs the field**: the sheet can offer an advance, not an out.

The section heading stops being "GOT AWAY" — that is about the ball, and a steal is not.

### L — delete `RunnerAdvanceReason.AWARDED` (§4.3)

No writer, and no use case. Mark: *"anytime a base is awarded, there's a reason other than just
because."* Proven by the corpus: play_07 and play_12 both describe awards in their titles and both
carry a **specific** reason (`catcher_interference`, `obstruction`). `enabledByCallId` — "RuleCall
that awarded this advance" — already links an award to its ruling.

A §21.5 schema change: minimal text edit to `runner_advance.schema.json`, regenerate both targets, one
atomic commit. Never round-trip the file.

## Explicitly out of scope

- **The possession cue** (who has the ball reads as a hairline ring) — **tabled, not M1**
  (Mark, 2026-08-30). DIA-018 owns it; it is a design problem rather than a defect, and the pulse
  idea is scrapped on battery.
- **Discoverability of optional entry actions** — post-M1 design ticket.
- **Schema fields with no writer** — `actualType`, `contactQuality`, `velocity`, `note`, `fielderId`,
  `effectiveAfter`. Tabled; none of them makes a play score wrongly. `snapshot` is DIA-009's.
- **Whether `bounceLocation` earns its tap** — open product question, post-M1. Slice A gives it a
  second real use, which is worth knowing when that question is finally answered.
- **Editing history at all** — correcting a sealed plate appearance, inserting a missed event,
  deleting one. **DIA-020**, post-M1. Slice F is the guard that makes undo stop at the door; DIA-020
  is the door.
- **What `✕` means on a between-pitch entry.** On a batted ball it restarts the play; on an idle
  entry there is no first question to return to, so it closes the field. Defensible, but it is one
  control with two meanings and DIA-018's field design should confirm it.
- **The rest of the design's top bar.** `Pitch Screen.dc.html` also carries the score and a
  base-occupancy diamond, which the shipped HUD does not. Belongs with DIA-018.
- **The hit-vs-error reform** — deleting `ordinaryEffort` and asking how far the batter earned.
  **DIA-022**, M1. It is the same change as slice J arriving from the other side, but it touches the
  schema, §13.2 and seven acceptance fixtures, so it is not a slice here.
- **The pitch-entry rail** — the three-step call/relay/location ladder, the labeled top-bar undo, and
  per-screen `Discard pitch`. **DIA-021**, M1.
- **Plays deferred on an honest fallback — and the reasoning that was wrong.** An enum audit found
  several rules recordable in the schema and unreachable in the app, and they were first deferred on
  *frequency*: look-back is called a few times a season and mostly at 11/12, leaving early is rarely
  called. **That was the wrong test** (Mark, 2026-09-03): frequency argues about what to build
  *first*, never about what to make available, and a rule called twice a season still has to be
  enterable those two times. Both moved into slice H. What is genuinely deferred is only where the
  **fallback is honest**: an infield fly enters as a catch, "which it usually is anyway," and an
  illegal pitch "could just be scored a ball," which is what the count effect already does. So
  `RunnerAdvanceReason.ILLEGAL_PITCH` and the `CallType` tail stay out — not because they are rare,
  but because nothing is misrecorded without them.
- **Runner hit by a batted ball** (`How.BATTED_BALL_CONTACT`) — rare like the others, "a couple of
  times per season," but the **only one whose fallback misattributes both halves**: the batter loses
  a hit she earned and the runner's out gets a wrong `how`. It also has a passing §14 fixture
  (play_13) that no UI path can produce. Left open rather than filed; not decided on frequency alone.
