## Slices

### A — Location backfill after a dropped third strike (§11.1, §11.3)

Approved 2026-08-30. §11.1 v0.39's "record last pitch" offer already exists in full
(`RecordLastPitchOffer`, `PitchStep.recordLast`, backfill as a §6 correction). It is armed only for
`outcome == IN_PLAY`, which is narrower than the spec's own principle: §11.1 says a pitch missing its
location can be given one at any time, and the *rationale* is that on contact the scorer's attention
leaves the zone. A D3K is that same situation — the batter is running.

1. Arm `lastPitchOffer` on `IN_PLAY || d3k != null`, both location fields still null
   (`pitch_flow.dart`, in `commitOutcome`).
2. **The bounce hinge on the recordLast canvas.** Mark: "have to have bounce available there." A
   D3K's signature case is a pitch in the dirt, so without it the slice half-works on the case that
   motivates it. `onCommitBounce` is already an optional `ZoneCanvas` param; add a
   `recordLastBounce` writing `bounceLocation` into the same §6 correction. Backfill becomes the
   same canvas as live entry — only what the release writes differs.
3. `pitch_loop_page.dart`'s "No bounce hinge: the offer exists only for in-play pitches" goes stale.
4. Tests: the offer stands after each of the four D3K endings and after the `d3kToField` round trip;
   taking it lands the correction; the bounce path writes `bounceLocation` and not `actualLocation`.
5. Spec bump, three places.

Nearly free, because the four D3K resolutions and `d3kToField` already reset with
`PitchFlowState(lastPitchOffer: state.lastPitchOffer)` — a standing offer survives them with no
plumbing.

**Decided:** do *not* arm it for every unlocated pitch. A scorer who routinely skips location would
get the affordance on nearly every call screen; in-play and D3K share a justification that takes and
balls do not. And it arms at commit, so it stands regardless of how the D3K ended — out on the throw
included.

### B — Undo should not discard the call and the location (§11.3)

Mark: *"if we record a pitch, say a ball, and then click undo, we're undoing the location and the
call as well. This is wrong."*

The call, the actual location, and the outcome are held in `PitchFlowState` and written as one
`PitchThrown`. `undoLast` voids that event, which is right for the *stream* — but it also throws away
two pieces of entry the scorer never wanted to redo. Mistap "Ball" for "Called strike" and you
re-enter the wristband code and the zone to fix a wrong button.

The data needed is in the voided payload. Undo of a just-committed pitch should void it, repopulate
`PitchFlowState` from that payload (`intendedType`, `intendedZoneId`, `intendedLocation`,
`actualLocation`, `bounceLocation`), and return to the **outcome step** so the scorer re-answers only
the question they got wrong.

**Decided (Mark, 2026-08-30):** consequences the loop wrote itself — the automatic strikeout out, a
walk's forced chain — stay voided. *"If it happened automatically, then one undo should undo
everything that happened automatically."* So the undo unit is unchanged; what changes is only that
the scorer's own entry is handed back to her instead of thrown away. Nothing she typed is
automatic, and nothing automatic survives.

### C — Step undo on the field draft (§15.2) — **PARKED**

**Mark, 2026-08-30: needs more time in the simulator before this is decided.** Not scheduled, and
not counted against this ticket's accept until he calls it. Written down so the shape survives.

The same mismatch as B, on the other surface: "undo the last action" and "back up one step" are
different operations. `reset()` (the unlabeled ↺) throws the whole draft away and `removeEntry(key)`
removes one node the scorer can point at, but there is no "take back what I just did" — and what she
just did is often a *batch* (a throw writes a touch and a leg together), which is exactly what a
per-node removal cannot express.

Snapshot-based: push the draft before each mutating action, pop on step undo. Sizing this is part of
the slice.

### D — A way to choose dark mode (§23.1.4)

Both themes are built and tested, and nothing selects between them: there is no `ThemeMode` anywhere
in `lib/` and no settings surface. The app follows the system, which is the wrong default for the
use — a tablet in a dugout at a 9pm game is not reliably in system dark, and the scorer cannot fix it
from inside the app.

Smallest honest version: a persisted three-state preference (system / light / dark) and somewhere to
tap it. No new design work; the themes exist.

### E — "Fielded" and "Picked it up" (§15.2) — **mostly already true**

**Decided (Mark, 2026-08-30):** both words survive, classified together. *"After you drop a fly
ball, you don't really field it, you do pick it up. However, does that matter for our purposes?
Either way, you take possession of it."* It does not matter: the distinction is linguistic, not
physical, and §13 charges on the physical fact.

That is **already what the code does** — the labels are context-gated and both write
`TouchType.FIELDED`, so nothing downstream can tell them apart:

| branch | label |
|---|---|
| airborne, in flight | `Picked it up` |
| ground ball | `Fielded` |
| `!battedBall` (between pitches) | `Fielded` |

**The one thing to fix:** the third row. A catcher chasing a wild pitch to the backstop is the same
"ball on the ground, take possession" case as the dropped fly, so by the rule above it should read
`Picked it up`. The comment immediately above that entry already says "she picked it up" while the
label says otherwise. A one-line relabel, no type change.

### F — Undo stops at the plate appearance (§6, §11.3)

Decided 2026-08-31, out of the conversation slice B started. B fixed *what undo throws away*; this
fixes *how far back it reaches*, and the two are the same button.

**The rule:**

> A plate appearance **seals** when the next one begins, and stays sealed. Undo reaches anything
> unsealed.

"Begins" is **calling the next pitch** — the first type tap — or, when the call is skipped, the
first action belonging to that pitch. That signal is not invented here: the record-last-pitch offer
already dies on exactly it, and says so at `pitch_loop_page.dart:118`: *"the loop moving on is
itself the dismissal, and starting the next call IS moving on: the first type tap removes it."*
This generalizes that to undo.

Sealing is a **high-water mark**, and that is the load-bearing part. An earlier draft of the rule
put the floor at "the start of the plate appearance containing the most recent action," which
bounds nothing: peel a PA empty and the most recent action moves into the previous one, taking the
floor with it, all the way to the first pitch of the game. Monotonic sealing does not move
backwards — undo everything in PA 6 and PA 5 stays sealed, because PA 6 having started is a fact
about history, not about the current state.

**Why it is worth doing.** Every hazard around editing history — shifted counts, a PA that ended
earlier than recorded, later pitches belonging to a different batter — needs undo to cross a PA
boundary. Confine it inside one and none of them are reachable by the everyday button. Undo stops
meaning "rewind the game" and means "take back what I just did," which is what a scorer means by it.
Going further back is DIA-020's job, deliberately through a different surface.

**It contradicts the spec, so it carries a bump.** §6 ("Instant, unlimited depth") and §11.3
("Undo is always one tap, top-level, unlimited depth") both promise the opposite. This is a revision,
not a defect fix.

**It also revises a passing test.** `pitch_loop_acceptance_test` peels three levels *across* PA
boundaries on purpose — undo #2 is commented "opp-2's PA reopens" and undo #3 takes the previous
batter's walk. Under the seal rule undo #3 is refused. DIA-009's manual script inherits that same
sequence, which is the concrete reason this lands **before** DIA-009 rather than after.

**The seal cannot be derived from the stream.** The call lives in `callDraftProvider` and nothing is
written until the outcome commits, so "she started calling the next pitch" leaves no trace. Live,
`callDraftProvider.pitchTypeId != null` is the signal. After a relaunch the draft is empty and the
app cannot know, so **a restart seals**: undo reaches only into the current incomplete PA. Closing
the app right after a strikeout costs the ability to undo it, which errs toward protecting history,
and a restart is itself a strong "moved on."

**No prerequisite, and an earlier draft of this ticket was wrong to claim one.** It said the floor
needed plate-appearance boundaries lifted out of `official_scoring.dart`, sharing work with DIA-009's
box score. It does not. The boundary is a `batterId` change on `PitchThrown` —
`official_scoring.dart:399` detects it with nothing more than that — and `undoLast` already walks the
visible stream backwards, so it stops on the same change in a few lines. DIA-009 needs plate
appearance and at-bat *totals*, which is a different need; "both involve plate appearances" was
mistaken for "both need the same code."

**Ships inside slice B's PR, not its own.** F's refusal needs the undo button to *show* it is
unavailable, and B rewired that call site — split apart, one PR makes the button do something new
and the other makes it sometimes decline, with the disabled state landing awkwardly in neither. They
are also the same function and would collide. Kept as separate sections here because the reasoning
behind each is worth keeping distinct.
