# DIA-018 — The field surface, designed
**Spec:** §15.1–§15.6, §16.3, §18.7 → §23 (design language), §23.1.4, §23.1.7
**Sequencing note:** **M1** (Mark, 2026-11-05), and **before DIA-021** — 021b cannot take the `✕` out
of the top bar until the field has its own control back, and this ticket designs that control. It was
written as not-M1, on the grounds that nothing here scores a play wrongly; CLAUDE.md's M1 list already
carried it from the 2026-09-02 widening, and this note now agrees. The DIA-016 stack it waited on has
landed. The four defects below are all things Mark hit while actually using the app.
**Goal:** the field surface has never had a design pass. DIA-008 built it functionally with no design
input; DIA-016 built the palette, type, metrics and components and applied them to the loop. The field
got the tokens and none of the attention.

## This is not a bucket for anything that looks wrong

Worth stating first, because the ticket will attract them. On 2026-08-28 Mark found white-on-white
chips in the trajectory dialog. That was **not** a design problem — `ChipThemeData.labelStyle` had been
handed a colorless `TextStyle`, and the fix was one line plus the golden that should have existed.
Rendering bugs are bugs; they get found by goldens and fixed where they live.

What belongs here is the part no correctness fix reaches: whether a scorer can *see what to do* and
*tell what a control will do to her play*.

## Four defects already queued, all found by using it

**Possession is a hairline.** The holder renders as a 2px accent ring at r=19 around a 15px circle
(`field_painter.dart`). Mark: too weak.

**A pulse is decided against and off the table** (Mark, 2026-08-28): *"battery life is a real issue out
there during a weekend tournament. We prob should scrap that idea completely."* That closes it on the
firmer of the two arguments. §18.7's ban on decorative motion is a rule about taste, and rules about
taste get overridden by someone who thinks this case is different; **a device that has to survive a
Saturday and a Sunday with nobody charging it is a runtime constraint**, and the idle field is up
between every pitch, so continuous animation means an always-on repaint for most of a tournament. The
weaker arguments still hold and are worth keeping for the next time motion is proposed: the ring
already carries the fact, so a pulse adds emphasis rather than information — and motion has phases,
with the low phase *less* legible than a strong static state, which fights sunlight-glanceable.

**So the remaining question is which static treatment**, and it is a real one: fill the holder's token
in the accent rather than ringing it; thicken the ring; reserve a hue for possession; or draw a small
ball glyph at her position — literal rather than symbolic, the direction panel 4A took with its
numbered badges.

**It matters most on the idle field** (§15.6), where possession is *seeded* rather than earned and the
scorer has no memory of a play to reason from.

**And the ball marker reads as possession when a fielder is standing on it.** Mark, 2026-09-04, from
the simulator: he dragged a fielder to a ground ball, chose *Missed it*, and read the result as that
fielder holding the ball. Nothing was recorded — the marker was simply sitting at the landing spot he
had dragged her to, which is the one place the two are guaranteed to coincide. So the cue has to
distinguish *the ball is here* from *she has it* at zero separation, which rules out the small ball
glyph on its own. §15.1 v0.56 makes this sharper rather than softer: possession is now Diamond's
stated belief and one tap corrects it, so the belief has to be legible before the scorer can know
whether it needs correcting.

**Two unlabeled destructive controls sit side by side.** ↺ (start the play over) and ✕ (discard) are
bare icons, and Flutter tooltips do not appear on touch — so on a tablet there is no way to tell them
apart before pressing one. Mark asked for step-undo *as if it were new*, which is the evidence: the
restart already exists and he could not find it. **The Claude Design panels do not solve this** — 4A
and 5A both put the same bare `↺ ✕` pair in the status bar. What they *do* label is the constructive
action ("✓ Commit play"), which is the inconsistency backwards from ours.

**Nothing announces the optional actions.** §15.1's grammar has a required spine — trajectory, landing,
the ✓ — and a set of refinements that enrich the record and block nothing: the second tap for where the
ball ended, arrival-quality chips, retyping a touch, re-attributing a leg,
and the ⚖ rule calls, which §15.3 deliberately hides. *"Found where it is looked for"* only works if the
scorer knows to look. Mark: *"we may need some helper language to tell the user what he can possibly
do. We don't have to get where it was retrieved, but we can."*

**Undo throws away the call and the location.** Undoing a committed pitch discards the intent and the
coordinates with it, forcing full re-entry. The right behavior is returning to the outcome step with
the entry intact.

## The mocks exist and have never been read against the built surface

Claude Design's Turn 06 produced panels **6A–6H**, and four are field surfaces: *off the bat, runner
advance, play editor, ground view*. DIA-016 used them **only** to audit color and type values, and
nothing has consumed their layout. Auditing them against what DIA-008 actually built is the first
slice's real work — the same exercise DIA-016 did for the palette, where reading the panels turned up
a third text tier and two surface steps the table never named.

**Read them for what they got wrong, too.** The `↺ ✕` finding above is exactly that: Mark remembered
the designs handling it better and they do not. A mock is evidence about looks, never about the model
— the mocks have not read the spec.

## The drag has no way to say "not that" (2026-09-04)

Two findings from the DIA-022 simulator round, both about the pills that appear at a base during a
drag.

**Dragging a fielder to a base's OUT pill does nothing, and should record the out.** Mark tried it to
enter an unassisted force at third — the natural motion, since the third baseman is standing on the
bag — and got a silent reposition instead. The cause is that `_paintBaseTargets` raises the SAFE/OUT
pair only while a **runner token** is dragged or a force play is pending, so a fielder drag has
nothing to drop on. Gate the new gesture on possession: she cannot retire anyone without the ball,
and gating it also keeps the pair off every ordinary "go get it" drag. Note it overlaps the existing
way in — dragging the *runner's* token to the same pill — so this is two gestures for one event and
wants deciding once, not twice.

**Dragging her must never give up the ball; only tapping her may.** Mark, 2026-09-04. This is already
how it works — the release is gated on `!moved`, where `moved` is `distance > kTouchSlop` — so the
new gesture simply has to stay on the drag path, and the rule is recorded here because it is easy to
break without noticing. The edge to watch is a drag *shorter* than the touch slop, roughly 18 logical
pixels: a fielder already standing near the bag is a short pull from its OUT pill, and that pull
would read as a tap and drop the ball instead of recording the out. The consequence is invisible when
it happens — this is precisely the confusion that sent us looking for a throw-grammar bug that did
not exist: a re-tap on the third baseman had silently dropped the ball, and the possession ring was
too faint to say so. A silent destructive gesture and an illegible possession cue compound, which is
an argument for settling 018a's cue before 018d's gesture rather than after.

**A pill should get out of the way when it is plainly not wanted.** Mark: hovering over one for a
couple of seconds should hide it, "because it's not what the user wants", and dragging away and back
should bring it back. The pills own field space, and sometimes the space is what the scorer needs —
to drop a fielder where she actually played the ball, for instance. Dismiss only the pill dwelt on,
not the pair. **The risk to watch is that dwelling is also what aiming looks like**: if it fires
while someone is lining up an OUT, the dwell duration is the dial.

## Ruled 2026-11-05 (from Turn 12's review)

- **Rule calls stay where §15.3 puts them:** on the runner-consequence chips in the play chain,
  *the batter's included* (batter-runner interference). Not on the runner rows (Turn 12 drew a
  + CALL there) and never as a standing button.
- **Dwell-to-hide completes or it did not happen.** The pill hides only once the full hold
  completes. Any release before that is an ordinary release and commits whichever pill is lit, so
  aiming slowly at OUT still records the out. The hold's progress fill is functional, not
  decoration.
- **Both gestures for one out are kept.** Dragging the holder onto OUT (gated on possession) and
  dragging the runner onto OUT record the same events. The first is what the scorer reaches for
  when the fielder is at the bag; the second covers outs where she is not.
- **No runner panel under the field.** Turn 12 drew rows of base buttons per runner; nobody asked
  for them, the spec has none, and runners resolve by drag. Left out (Turn 14, 14A).
- **018b's discoverability is the 11D rule alone for M1:** solid means required and dashed means
  optional, with each extra placed as a dashed target on the thing it modifies. Helper text waits
  until the DIA-009 run shows the scorer still missing something.

## Out of scope

Anything that scores a play differently — that is DIA-015's remit and it is finished. The batter figure
(DIA-013). Defensive positioning — setting fielders before the play, with presets, carried between
at-bats — which is a feature, not a design pass.

**One finding held for that feature (2026-09-04):** *Go to field* does nothing before the first pitch
of a half-inning — `betweenPitchAnchors()` returns no pitch id and the handler silently returns —
because §15.6's between-pitch entry hangs its chain off the pitch that already exists. Positioning is
by definition **pre**-pitch, so it cannot inherit that gate. §15.6 gives this surface two jobs and
only one is built: recording a runner event genuinely needs an anchor, while *seeing the field* is
something the spec says the coach can do **any time**. Splitting them is the positioning work's first
problem, not a detail of it. The silent `return` is its own small wrong — a button that does nothing
tells you nothing.
