# DIA-032 — The play chain strip, designed (§15.2, §15.3, §13.5)
**Spec:** §15.2 (the strip), §15.3 (the modifier chip), §13.5 (overrides, entered with the play,
v0.76), §18.7 (design language), §23 (color)
**Design:** Turn 16 on the live canvas (`docs/design/README.md`): 16A–16C are the three directions,
16D is the review at the ✓, and 16E is the menu language (2026-11-24).
**Sequencing note:** **M1.** DIA-010's override entry point waits on this, and with it Mark's third
M1 clause, *the scorer can override*.

## Why

Mark, 2026-11-24: *"we have a whole string of parts of the play in the middle of the screen. the
text is really small. it's kind of weird and unclear what it does. you can't even tell that these
events are clickable where other stuff can be entered. the popups that do appear seem to lack
design."* And: *"Think big on the design — don't rule any option off the table — it doesn't have to
work like it currently is if something else would be better."*

## Audit of the build (2026-11-24)

**The strip** (`play_chain_strip.dart`):

- Nodes are 40 px grey circles with a 13 px position label (`SS`, `RF`), on the same grey as the
  page. Nothing reads as a button.
- Consequences are 13 px chips in a code-like notation: `B→1`, `3→⌂`, `B✕2`.
- Every node and consequence carries a dashed `+ misplay` / `+ call · re-link` chip, at 12 px with
  2 px of padding. These are the only affordance, and they are the smallest type on the screen.
- **`⚾` and `⌂` are missing from the bundled fonts** and render as missing-glyph boxes in the
  goldens. Check the device before trusting either.
- It reads as a log of what was entered, not as the play. A scorer has to translate `B→1 + call ·
  re-link` into "the batter was safe at first", which is the job the play log's sentences already
  do well.

**The popups** (node menus):

- Stock Material `ListTile`s and `ChoiceChip`s in a plain dialog. They don't follow 14B's dialog
  language, and the selected chip is a grey check, not the accent.
- Questions are titled inconsistently (*SS — what happened?* against bare lists).

**Known problems on this surface, already on file:**

- **Discoverability of optional actions:** retrieval point, arrival quality, retype, re-link, ⚖
  calls. *"We may need some helper language to tell the user what he can possibly do."*
- **The possession cue is too weak** (a 2 px ring).
- **A wild throw retypes the wrong node:** the eye goes to the receiver, while the throw belongs to
  the thrower.
- **The loose-ball vocabulary between pitches is wrong:** a catcher cannot boot a wild pitch.
- **"Fielded" and "Picked it up"** are two labels for one touch type.

**What is new:** the run's earned / RBI rulings (DIA-010) need a home inside the play.

## Directions to draw

All on the table, per Mark. Each one is a different answer to *where does the scorer read and
amend the play while entering it*.

1. **The strip, made legible.** Same model, redrawn:
   - nodes as real buttons with a verb (*SS · booted*);
   - consequences as runner chips in words (*B safe at 1st*);
   - one visible "more" affordance per node instead of a dashed chip on everything;
   - every menu in 14B's language.
2. **The play as sentences.** Replace the strip with a live readback: the play log's grammar, while
   the play is being entered. Each sentence's parts are tappable: *SS* opens the fielder, *booted*
   opens the misplay set, *b1 scored* opens earned / RBI. The model stays atomic events; only the
   reading changes.
3. **Edit on the field, review at the ✓.** No strip. Every fielder and runner on the canvas is its
   own control (DIA-018d already does this for receivers). The ✓ opens a short review card: the play
   in sentences, plus the judgments that belong to the whole play (which error, sacrifice, earned,
   RBI), each one tap to change.

These can combine. Direction 3's review card works with either of the first two, and §13.5's
rulings may sit best there whatever the strip becomes.

## Ruling (Mark, 2026-11-24 and 2026-11-25)

- **16A is the main view**: the strip made legible.
- **16D appears only when it's needed**: when the ✓ has a question open (reach, sacrifice). Earned
  and RBI never open it.
- **16E's menu styling is used app-wide** (§23.7), not just on the chain.
- **16C is discarded** (too busy) and removed from the canvas.
- **16B is tabled**: Mark will use the strip without the sentences first. Not ruled out.

Spec v0.77 carries all of this (§13.5, §15.2–§15.5, §23.7).

## Slices

One branch and one PR each, each with its own plan first.

1. **032a — one dialog component (§23.7)**, and every popup in the app moved onto it.
2. **032b — the strip redrawn (§15.2)**: steps as buttons with a verb, runner rows in words,
   sideways scroll; ⚾ and ⌂ go.
3. **032c — the run's row and menu (§13.5)**: earned and RBI, written in the play's batch. This
   closes DIA-010 and Mark's M1 override clause.
4. **032d — the review card at the ✓ (§15.5)**, replacing the serial reach and sacrifice dialogs.
