# DIA-016 — Design foundations: the real palette, typography, and component definitions
**Spec:** §18.7 (design language), §23.1–23.3 (color contexts, semantic reservations)
**Sequencing note:** M1-adjacent. Gates the design half of **DIA-014** and any later screen work —
every surface currently renders on Flutter defaults with placeholder color. Does **not** gate
DIA-009; the acceptance script cares about behavior, not paint.
**Goal:** replace DIA-012's placeholders with Diamond's actual brand, add the typography that has
never existed, and define the component vocabulary — button, modal, chip, card, spacing, radii — that
screens are currently inventing one call site at a time.

## What already exists, and what doesn't

**DIA-012 built the seam and said so:** *"Every color in this file is a placeholder and is expected to
be thrown away. What is not provisional is the seam."* `brand_baseline.dart` is the only file in
`app/lib/src/ui/` permitted to hold color literals — enforced by `no_color_literals_test.dart` — and
every widget already reads `Theme.of(context)`. `DiamondSemantics` exists as a `ThemeExtension`.
**None of that needs rebuilding.**

**Typography does not exist at all.** No `TextTheme`, no font family — every surface is on Flutter's
defaults. Unlike color, both the pipe and the values are missing.

**Component definitions do not exist.** The symptom is `field_dialog.dart`, which hand-scales its own
chip, button, and list-tile themes at a single call site because there was nothing shared to reach
for. Every popup on the field surface inherits from that one function by accident of history.

## The brand: 1A "Infield", light and dark

From Claude Design's `Diamond Branding.dc.html`. **1A is the build direction. 1B "Night Game" is a
separate identity and is not being used** — including as a dark theme, since its mark and voice
differ. Dark is **2A "Infield · dark"**, the same identity after sundown.

The dark theme is **not an inversion**. Ink is promoted from "the darkest color" to *the card*, with
Field below it and one raised step above:

| role | light | dark |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ground | `#E7E5DE` | `#0E1613` |
| Card | `#FFFEF9` | `#16211C` |
| Raised | `#F4F2EB` | `#1E2C25` |
| Hairline | `#D6D3C8` | `#2A3A32` |
| Text | `#16211C` | `#F4F2EB` |
| Text, muted | `#8A8A80` | `#A9C0AF` |
| Grass, as line | `#2E5E3E` | `#5FA97A` |
| Clay, as line | `#B4643C` | `#D07E4E` |

**The rule that makes this small:** Grass and Clay keep their light-theme values wherever they are a
**fill** with Chalk sitting on top — a `#2E5E3E` button is correct in both themes. The lifted values
are **only** for strokes, icons, and text, where the light values disappear against Ink.

**Two more inversion rules, from Turn 06:** *"What was an Ink panel becomes a raised panel; a small
Ink chip becomes Chalk with Ink glyphs, since a chip needs to win against the card it sits on."* Size
governs again — a large Ink surface steps up to Raised, a small Ink chip inverts to Chalk.

**The four-step ladder is a floor, not the whole set.** Auditing 6A–6H turns up values the table does
not name, several of them heavily used:

| value | uses | what it is |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `#8FA396` | 85 | a **third text tier**, between muted `#A9C0AF` and dim `#6E7C74` — team names in the scoreboard |
| `#FFFEF9` | 30 | label text sitting **on a brand fill** ("In play", "PASSED BALL") — near-white, not Chalk |
| `#26352E` | 17 | a surface step above Raised |
| `#4A5C52` | 7 | a fourth, dimmest text tier — the score separator |

Rationalize these into named tiers rather than shipping the four-step table and letting screens
invent the rest. **Two values need identifying before they land anywhere:** `#6E8FA8` (blue) and
`#C9A277` (tan), both used as fills. If they are the two teams in the mock, they belong to §23.3's
**accent tier and not the brand baseline** — which is explicitly out of scope here.

**Typography: Space Grotesk (400/500/700) everywhere, JetBrains Mono (400/500/700) for counts, codes
and positions.** The brand note gives the reason and it is a constraint, not a preference: *"two faces
with the same squared skeleton, so the app reads as one system."*

**The mark** is a chalk-line diamond — one stroke weight, one angle, a 45°-rotated square outline in
Grass with home plate anchoring the bottom. App icon: 88px, 20px radius, Grass ground, Chalk diamond,
Clay plate. Tagline **"Coach the whole field."** In dark the mark inverts to Grass-lit outline with a
Chalk plate; the **dugout sign stays Chalk in both themes — it is a printed object.**

## The reserved colors (§23.2)

Three states, three colors, none of them shared. Sized to how each actually appears.

| name | light | dark | meaning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Dusk** | `#5A47A0` | `#9B85E8` | uncertainty — the count may be wrong |
| **Rosin** | `#E8A81C` | `#F2B935` | misplay — fault, not yet adjudicated |
| **Ejection** | `#BB1E3C` | `#F4667B` | the app failed |

**Dusk is violet because nothing on a ball field is** — it cannot be misread as grass, dirt, or
blood, and it reads *unresolved* rather than *bad*, which is correct: an ambiguous count is not an
error and should not alarm the coach for four minutes.

**Rosin is amber, and amber works here and only here.** A 40px chip needs maximum chroma to register
at a glance, and its separation from Clay is **lightness plus saturation, not hue** — Clay is dark
and muted, Rosin is bright and pure, which is the most robust pair of cues in direct sun.
**Rosin is fill-only, never text or line**: amber type on Chalk is illegible outdoors. The chip
carries Ink glyphs in both themes, so one value serves noon and floodlights.

**Ejection is pushed off pure red toward magenta** so it clears Clay by hue as well as chroma. It is
reserved for **the app failing and never for a fielding error** — two phrases that mean opposite
things in this product, which is exactly why they cannot share a color.

**Size governs treatment.** Chroma is what separates a color from Clay, but large areas of high
chroma are unreadable after two innings. So the persistent 84px HUD gets a **tinted field with a
saturated label** (≈8% chroma across the field, full strength on the 34px label and the count
separator), while the 40px chip gets a **fully saturated fill**.

**Contrast is verified.** Every pair checked passes WCAG AA — Rosin chip 7.9, Dusk label 7.3,
Ejection 6.2, dark muted text 8.6, lifted Grass and Clay as line on card 5.9 and 5.3. The one to
watch is `#8A8A80` muted text on Chalk at **3.45**: fine at the label sizes it is used for, not for
body copy.

## `callable` is Grass, not a fourth reserved color

`DiamondSemantics.callable` shades which zones the call grid can call. **It is not an event** — the
other three are things that just happened; callable is a *standing description of where the tool
works*, true of most zones most of the time. A dedicated hue would put permanent alert weight across
half the grid, and two greens a few degrees apart is a worse collision risk than anything the
reserved colors pose.

So it is **one hue in three steps** — availability, selection, exclusion:

| step | means | light | dark |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| wash | callable | Grass @ 14% fill, @ 34% border | Grass-lit @ 18% fill, @ 42% border |
| solid | called | Grass fill, Chalk code | Grass-lit fill, Field code |
| dashed | off | card + dashed hairline | card + dashed hairline |

The wash is **fill and border**, not fill alone — the border is what keeps a 14% wash reading as a
cell rather than a smudge in sunlight. The called cell carries the wristband code (§10.1).

Retire `callableGreen` and point the slot at Grass with an opacity step. As the design puts it, the
collision *is* the point: three steps of one hue say availability, selection and exclusion. What it
must never be is a fourth **value** drifting a few degrees off Grass — two greens, neither of which
looks intentional.

Worth keeping as the general rule: **a reserved color is for an event, not a state.** Anything
standing and common gets a step on an existing hue.

## Two constraints

**The fonts must be bundled, not fetched.** The design pulls Space Grotesk and JetBrains Mono from
Google Fonts via `<link>`. **Offline-first is the prime directive** (§12.6, §19.1, §21.5): no feature
may require connectivity to record or view anything during a game, and a dugout has no signal. Ship
the files in the bundle and declare them in `pubspec.yaml` — the commented template is already there.
A runtime fetch, including the `google_fonts` package's default behavior, is a build failure waiting
for a Saturday with no bars. Both faces ship through Google Fonts under open licenses, so bundling is
permitted; static weights are simpler than variable and enough for a fixed scale.

**The count HUD is a restructure, not a swap.** Today it turns amber wholesale. The new treatment
tints the field and saturates only the label and the count separator, which means `count_hud.dart`
changes shape rather than changing a token.

## Extract the scale, don't invent one

The panels already encode a type scale and a spacing rhythm consistently across four artboards
(1A, 2A, 4A, 5A). **Read them out rather than designing a second one**, then rationalize — the goal
is the smallest set that covers what the panels do, not a token per observed value.

**Type.** Recurring sizes: 44 (wordmark, and the count in the HUD) · 38/32/30 (the outcome sheet's
primaries and its title) · 26 (section headings, dugout sign) · 21/19 (secondary and rare chips) ·
17/15 (titles and body) · 13/12.5 (notes). Display sizes carry negative tracking (`-0.045em` on the
wordmark, `-0.02em` on headings); body does not.

**The mono rule is wider than "numbers."** In the panels JetBrains Mono also carries every
uppercase letter-spaced label — eyebrows, section tags, chip glyphs, "COUNT UNSURE" — at `0.08em` to
`0.22em` tracking, sizes 8.5–14. So the rule is closer to: **mono for anything read as a code rather
than as prose** — counts, positions, wristband codes, distances, and small caps labels. Prose is
Space Grotesk at every size.

**Spacing and radii.** The rhythm is a 4px base: gaps of 6/8/10/12/14/18/20/24/28, panel padding
20/24/28, page 56. Radii cluster at 8/12/14/16/20/28/32 — chips and tooltips small, cards and sheets
large, the app icon at 20. **Component heights are the load-bearing set**, because they are touch
targets: 34 (label pill) · 40 (position chip) · 44 (list row) · 60 (rare chip) · 74 (modifier row) ·
84 (HUD) · 118 (primary outcome) · 132 (In play). Nothing interactive is below 40, which should
become the stated minimum.

## Deliverables

- **Palette** — both ladders, the three reserved colors, and Grass's opacity steps, all in
  `brand_baseline.dart`; `no_color_literals_test` must still pass.
- **Typography** — bundled fonts, a `TextTheme` scale, and a documented rule for when a number takes
  the mono face. Counts, codes, positions, and distances are mono; prose is not.
- **Components** — filled/outlined/text buttons, the centered modal (`field_dialog.dart`'s hand-rolled
  scaling folds into it), choice/filter/action chips, and the raised panel. Sized for a coach standing
  in a dugout with the sun on the screen, with a stated **minimum touch target**.
- **Spacing and radii tokens** — 5A and 4A use a consistent rhythm (28px panel padding, 16/12px radii,
  8px gaps) currently hardcoded per widget.
- **Elevation — the ladder does the work; shadows carry over unchanged.** Turn 06 settles this:
  6A reuses 5A's two shadows **identically** (`0 24px 60px rgba(22,33,28,0.28)` and the sheet's
  `0 -18px 44px rgba(0,0,0,0.3)`). Separation in dark comes from Field → Card → Raised, with the
  shadow left as it is. The one observed swap is the **app icon**, where 1A's
  `box-shadow: 0 2px 8px` becomes a `1px` hairline in 2A — an object standing alone rather than a
  raised surface. Define one shadow set, used in both themes.
- **App icon and splash** from the mark.
- **§23.2 rewritten** — it currently says amber means uncertainty. Uncertainty is Dusk; amber is
  misplay alone; Ejection is app failure and explicitly not a fielding error. Three-place protocol
  plus the two `CLAUDE.md` refs.

## Accept

- Every screen renders in Space Grotesk / JetBrains Mono with **networking disabled**.
- `no_color_literals_test` passes; no new literal outside `brand_baseline.dart`.
- Light and dark both render from the ladders, with fills unchanged across themes and only
  strokes/icons/text taking the lifted values.
- No shadow is used in dark, and no surface-step-plus-border stands in for elevation in light: each
  theme uses its own mechanism rather than one degrading into the other.
- Every golden has both variants; the suite already carries dark-mode goldens, so a theme regression
  should fail rather than go unrendered.
- Rosin never appears as text or line anywhere.
- Verified on device in daylight, not on a desk monitor, per §18.7's sunlight-glanceable bar.
- Goldens regenerated. **This changes every golden in the suite**, and the two field goldens already
  fail on CI for unrelated rasterization reasons — settle that first so a real regression is not
  hidden inside an expected mass update.

## A note on the screen mocks

**The dark mocks have landed** (Turn 06, panels 6A–6H): every light screen now has a counterpart —
pitch result, off the bat, runner advance, play editor, ground view, pitch record, pitcher game, and
batter scouting. **DIA-014 is unblocked.** This ticket was never waiting on them, but it gained from
them: the elevation correction and the extra surface and text tiers above both came out of reading
6A–6H against 5A.

Worth checking when those mocks arrive: whether type **weight** needs to drop a step in dark. Light
text on a dark ground reads optically bolder, and some systems compensate. The panels do not, so the
default is that it does not — but a full screen is where that becomes visible, not a swatch.
