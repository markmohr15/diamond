# DIA-024 — The scorer can see what she scored (§8, §15.4, §18.7)
**Spec:** §8 (projections roadmap: "Live scorebook / box score"), §13 (the derivations being read),
§15.4 (the play summary strip, named but never built), §18.7 → §23 (design language)
**Sequencing note:** **M1** (Mark, 2026-09-29), and **first**. It gates nothing, but it is the
instrument every other open ticket is verified with — see **Why it goes first**.
**Goal:** say, in a sentence, what each play scored as, and give the scorer somewhere to read them.
The sentence is the durable half — DIA-021 will render it a second time, as a confirmation step.

## Why

**Nothing in the app displays what a play scored.** `foldOfficialScoring` runs, derives correctly,
and no UI reads it: `batterOutcomes` and `outcomeFor` have zero consumers outside the fixture suite.
The scorer enters a play and gets no confirmation of what it became.

This was found the only way it could be. Through a full simulator session on 2026-09-04, checking
whether a play scored the way Mark meant required reading `diamond.sqlite` and folding the stream by
hand. Twice he asked *"no error recorded?"* and *"what happened on the last play"* about plays that
had scored **correctly** — the derivation was right and invisible. Once it was genuinely wrong, and
the only reason we found out is that someone read the database.

Two of M1's four clauses lean on this: *"where it cannot be right, the scorer can override"*
presumes she can tell that it is wrong, and *"a user could use it in a completely functional way"*
is hard to defend when she cannot see her own work.

## Why it goes first

The other open tickets — DIA-018, DIA-019's remaining slices, DIA-021, DIA-010 — are all verified by
entering plays and checking what happened. Without this, that check routes through someone reading
SQLite; with it, the scorer reads the app. The formatter is insulated from those tickets because it
reads **projection output**, not entry surfaces, so it does not churn as they land.

There is no collision with DIA-021: the log is **hidden by default and opened when wanted** (Mark,
2026-09-29), so it does not live on the screen the rail rebuilds. An earlier draft put a persistent
line on the call screen and accepted re-homing as a cost; making it a modal removes the cost rather
than paying it.

The line grows as work lands: **DIA-010's overrides must show**, or an overridden earned run is
invisible in exactly the way this ticket exists to fix.

## Confirmation is a different need, and DIA-021 owns it

A hidden log answers *"what did that score as?"* and cannot answer *"did that take what I meant?"* —
a surface you have to decide to open is one you will not open on the play that went wrong, which is
the condition this ticket exists to end. Nothing on 2026-09-04 said a word about a mis-scored play,
which is why it survived.

Mark's answer is better than the banner first proposed here: **derive the line as the play is
entered, or immediately after, and have the scorer confirm it when she finalizes the field entry.**
Confirmation becomes part of the ✓ instead of something to notice afterwards — and the ✓ is already
a gate, since DIA-022 made it ask the questions the record leaves open. Showing what those answers
produced belongs in the same breath.

**That lands in DIA-021's redesign, not here.** What this ticket owes it is **024a**: the formatter
is the shared dependency, and the confirm step and the log are two renderings of one sentence.

## The line

**A sentence per cause.** The batted ball is one cause; each charged misplay is another. This is a
**grouping over `enabledByTouchId`** rather than a pile of special cases: unattributed legs belong to
the batted ball's sentence, legs linked to a misplay belong to that misplay's sentence.

That is also what makes two misplays read as two errors — the thing DIA-022 settled at entry, now
said out loud:

> On a fielding error by 3B, Jones reached first. On a two base throwing error by 3B, Jones
> advanced to third.

Never one invented three-base error.

**Within a sentence: batter, action, fielder, then where the runners went if we have not already
said.** Mark's worked cases, which are the acceptance examples:

```
Jones doubled to left, Simmons advanced to third.
Jones grounded to SS, Simmons out at second on a fielder's choice, Jones to first.
Jones doubled to left, Simmons advanced to third.
On a fielding error by the LF, Simmons scored and Jones advanced to third.
```

**The verb splits two ways.** The hit when it was one — *singled, doubled, tripled, homered* — and
the trajectory when it was not: *grounded, lined, flied, popped, bunted* (`Trajectory` is
BUNT/FLY/GROUND/LINE/POPUP).

**The batter's destination is stated only when the verb has not already implied it.** *Doubled* says
she is on second, so nothing trails it; *grounded* says nothing, so the sentence closes with
`Jones to first`.

**Where the ball went** is the fielder's **abbreviation** when someone touched it — `to SS`, not "to
Shortstop" (Mark: it saves space and he reads SS as Shortstop) — and the **sector** when nobody did:
`to left`. Every batted ball says it, including a fielder's choice, a sacrifice fly and a sacrifice
bunt.

**Past tense throughout.**

## What it needs that does not exist

**A roster.** `LineupSet.battingOrder` is a list of opaque `playerId` strings and there is no
`game_start.schema.json` at all — §4 lists `GameStart` as carrying "teams, rosters" and it was never
schematized. So there are no names and no jersey numbers in the system, and today's line would read
*"opp-5 doubled to left."*

Identity resolves **name → jersey number → batting-order slot**. The third is derivable now, from the
`LineupSet` index, so the log is usable before a roster exists (`B5 doubled to left`) and improves
without changing the formatter. **The roster is a prerequisite for the first two and belongs to no
ticket.**

**Sector naming from a bearing.** Nothing derives *left field* or *the left-center gap* today.
§3.2's θ=0-at-CF is the input; `fenceDistanceAt(θ)` is currently the only bearing consumer.

## Between-pitch happenings belong in the log

Wild pitches, passed balls and stolen bases, in place between the plate appearances they happened
between. The inputs exist: `PitchGetaway` for WP/PB, `RunnerAdvance.reason == stolen_base` for
steals.

This makes the log's unit **something that happened** rather than **a plate appearance**, which
decides the projection's return shape — settle it before building rather than during.

## Slices

- **024a — the sentence.** Position abbreviations, sector naming from a bearing, the cause grouping,
  and the clause grammar, as a projection-side formatter. **Every §14 fixture gains an expected
  line** in its `expect` block: 19 plays already chosen to cover the weird cases, each becoming a
  string worth asserting. That is the test bed, and it follows §21.5 — fixtures are the definition of
  correct, never special cases in code.
- **024b — the log.** A modal, hidden by default, listing the half-inning most recent first, plus a
  golden. Renders 024a and computes nothing (§21.5: anything computing a stat outside the projection
  engine is a bug). Where it opens from is the loose end: the top bar is the obvious candidate and
  DIA-021 is rebuilding that bar, so settle it with the rail rather than ahead of it.

## Out of scope

A full box score — batting and pitching lines, LOB, totals. That is §17's filter model and its own
ticket.

**Editing from the strip is DIA-020, not this.** §15.4 names the play summary strip as where post-hoc
edits begin; building the display half now is deliberate, because it is the surface DIA-020 attaches
to.

**The roster itself.** Flagged above, owned by nobody, and needed before a name or a number can
appear.

## Accept

Every §14 fixture asserts its line; Mark's three worked cases render verbatim; a fielder's choice, a
sacrifice fly and a sacrifice bunt each say where the ball went; two misplays on one play read as two
sentences; a wild pitch and a stolen base appear in the log between the plate appearances they fell
between; and the scorer can open the log and read the half-inning.
