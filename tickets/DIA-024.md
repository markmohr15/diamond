# DIA-024 — The scorer can see what she scored (§8, §15.4, §18.7)
**Spec:** §8 (projections roadmap: "Live scorebook / box score"), §13 (the derivations being read),
§15.4 (the play summary strip, named but never built), §18.7 → §23 (design language)
**Sequencing note:** independent of everything open. It gates nothing and nothing gates it, but see
**Is this M1?** below — it is the one M1 question the ticket raises.

## Why

**Nothing in the app displays what a play scored.** `foldOfficialScoring` runs, derives correctly,
and no UI reads it: `batterOutcomes` and `outcomeFor` have zero consumers outside the fixture suite
and the tests. The scorer enters a play and gets no confirmation of what it became.

This was found the only way it could be. Through a full simulator session on 2026-09-04, checking
whether a play had scored the way Mark meant required reading `diamond.sqlite` and running the
stream through the projection by hand. Twice he asked "no error recorded?" and "what happened on the
last play" about plays that had in fact scored correctly — the derivation was right and invisible.
Once it was genuinely wrong, and the only reason we found out was that someone read the database.

That is fine for development and impossible for a user. Two of M1's four clauses lean on it:

- *"where it cannot be right, the scorer can override"* presumes she can tell that it is wrong.
- *"a user could use it in a completely functional way"* is hard to defend when the scorer cannot
  see what she scored.

Nothing scores incorrectly without this ticket, which is why it is a question rather than a given.

## The two halves

**A derivation.** Projection output into a readable line. Nothing needs storing: `BatterOutcome`
carries scoring/hit/rbi/atBat, `OfficialError` carries position/kind/basis, and an error's base count
falls out of the advance linked to it. This is the play description flagged during DIA-022 and parked
— Mark, 2026-09-04: *"batter reached first on a fielding error by SS. Batter reached third on a two
base throwing error by SS."* Two errors read as two sentences, never as one of N bases.

**A surface.** The half-inning's plays, most recent first, on the call screen — which is already the
idle surface between pitches and already hosts *Go to field*.

```
B5  single, 1 RBI — line drive to right field
B4  reached on a fielder's choice, ground ball to Shortstop — R1 out at second
    WP — R2 to third
B3  reached on E5 (throwing) — ground ball to Third
B2  HBP
```

## What the line has to say

**Where the ball went, on every batted ball.** Mark, 2026-09-28: a fielder's choice, a sacrifice fly
and a sacrifice bunt all still have to say where it was hit. *"Reached on a fielder's choice ground
ball to Shortstop - R1 out at second."* The phrase is `BallInPlay.trajectory` plus a place:

- **Touched** → the first touch's fielder. Needs full position names; only
  `positionAbbreviations` exists (`SS`), and "ground ball to SS" is not how it is said aloud.
- **Untouched** → the landing bearing's sector — *left field*, *the left-center gap*. Nothing
  derives sectors today; §3.2's θ=0-at-CF convention is the input and `fenceDistanceAt(θ)` is the
  only bearing consumer so far.

**Between-pitch happenings, not only plate appearances.** Wild pitches, passed balls and stolen bases
belong in the log. That makes the log's unit *something that happened* rather than *a plate
appearance* — a distinction worth settling before building, because it decides the projection's
return shape. The inputs exist: `PitchGetaway` for WP/PB, and `RunnerAdvance.reason == stolen_base`
for steals.

**Consequences after the em-dash.** Other runners out or scoring, and errors charged.

## Slices

- **024a — the sentence.** Position names, sector naming from a bearing, and the line itself, as a
  projection-side formatter. **Every §14 fixture gains an expected line** in its `expect` block: 19
  plays already chosen to cover the weird cases, and each becomes a string worth asserting. That is
  the test bed, and it follows the repo's rule that fixtures are the definition of correct rather
  than special cases in code.
- **024b — the log.** The list on the call screen, last entry readable without scrolling, plus a
  golden. Renders 024a; computes nothing (§21.5: anything computing a stat outside the projection
  engine is a bug).

## Out of scope

A full box score — batting and pitching lines, LOB, totals. That is §17's filter model and its own
ticket.

**Editing from the strip is DIA-020, not this.** §15.4 names the play summary strip as where
post-hoc edits begin, and building the display half now is deliberate: it is the surface DIA-020 will
attach to, so this is not throwaway work.

## Is this M1?

**Mark's call, and the ticket exists to put the question rather than answer it.** Including it widens
M1 a third time. By the letter of the bar it is polish — nothing scores wrongly without it. By the
fourth clause it is not, because a scorer who cannot see her own work cannot use the app
functionally, and cannot exercise the override the third clause promises.

## Accept

Every §14 fixture asserts its line; a fielder's choice, a sacrifice fly and a sacrifice bunt each say
where the ball went; a wild pitch and a stolen base appear in the log between the plate appearances
they happened between; and the scorer can read the half-inning without leaving the call screen.
