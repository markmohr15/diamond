The 3-digit code
7
0
2
Drop, low middle — on this game's card only.
The code is arbitrary, on purpose. Every type-and-location pair draws a random 3-digit number from the pitcher's armband card. Nothing in 702 hints at Drop or at low-middle, so the dugout across the field learns nothing from hearing it — the whole point of the armband. No digits on the pitch chips, no numbers on the grid.
It matches the armband. The app holds the same card the pitcher is wearing, so what the screen shows is what she reads off her wrist. Cards are per game (CARD 4 here) and reshuffle between games; the header names the active card so a coach can confirm the pitcher and the tablet agree before the first pitch.
Where it shows. Dead center under the pitch-type row — the first place the eye lands after tapping a type, at 62px so it reads from arm's length. The footer keeps a one-line confirmation, not a second copy of the digits.
Call was used. The checkbox under the code is the CALL→RECORD handoff: checking it logs 702 as actually thrown and advances to placing the real location, so called-vs-thrown is a fact in the data rather than an assumption. Left unchecked, the pitch records as thrown-off-call and the code is kept as the intent only.
Batter asset
ASSET SPEC
Source. Trace or knock out a single frame of a right-handed batter in a loaded stance, three-quarter rear view — the k-zone reference is the right read.
Format. Transparent PNG or SVG, single flat fill. No outline, no interior detail.
Framing. Crop tight: front foot at the bottom-right corner of the canvas, feet on the bottom edge. The screen anchors that corner 17 in from plate center and lets the rest run off the top and side. Export at 30:74 (e.g. 420 × 1036) — off-ratio art will need a nudge with Edit.
Handedness. One asset only — the left-handed view is a horizontal flip.
Drop the artwork straight onto the figure in the screen — it persists, flips with the BATS toggle, and scales with Tweaks → Batter size. The front foot stays pinned 17 in from plate center at any size, so the zone geometry never shifts.
What changed
One projection. Straight-on catcher's-eye elevation. The zone sits on the plate at real height (18–40 in) instead of floating over a perspective floor.
Batter cropped at the frame edge. A fillable slot, cropped at the top and side like a broadcast K-zone. He sits nearer the camera than the zone plane, so his size is a framing multiplier (Tweaks → Batter size), independent of the zone scale. Flips sides via BATS.
The dirt in front of the plate is a target. That band was always deliberate — a pitch that bounces short of the plate has a real location, and the 5×5 grid can't hold it. Tapping the dirt switches to a ground view looking down at the plate, where the ball is placed in front of, on, or behind it. It's labelled now so the tap is discoverable instead of a secret.
5×5 grid, not 3×3. The dashed outer ring captures off-zone pitches, which the old 3×3 couldn't record at all. Zone is 204 × 264 px with 68 × 88 px inner cells and 60 px outer cells — every target well clear of the 44 px floor.
Pitch history in place. Numbered dots show this at-bat; green strike / clay ball. The sequence is visible without leaving the screen.
Mode is explicit. CALL vs RECORD segmented control replaces the ambiguous "Record last pitch" pill + X.
Real primary action. 64 px filled button, bottom-right for two-handed reach, with a live summary beside it. Cancel/Skip are bordered, not bare links.
Daylight contrast. Chalk/ink/grass palette, 3 px zone stroke, 12 px mono labels at #7A7A70 or darker, no gray-on-gray.
Context restored. Batter, pitcher, pitch count, strike %, runners-on diamond, inning — all in 268 px of chrome.
Strike zone geometry is untouched — your 12 px/in, zone anchored on the plate at 18–40 in. Only the batter changed. The silhouette is a stand-in; a traced figure from real footage drops in at the same anchors, and the front foot stays pinned 17 in from plate center at any size.
