Notes on the play editor
The field is a surface, not a wireframe. Grass on the fair wedge, dirt on the infield, plate and bases in white — the same palette as the zone and dirt views. A hairline diamond on grey is invisible in daylight and gives the eye nothing to aim at.
Fielders are hittable. 54px tokens, dark type, real borders. Players in the play go solid green with a numbered badge, so the sequence is legible on the field itself — not only in a chip row.
One sequence, one place. The old screen split fielder chips and runner-advance chips into two tiny stacks near the top. Here the throw chain is a single row with an explicit + THROW, and runners get their own labelled rows where every possible ending base is a 44px target — no more two B→1 chips with no way to tell them apart.
Undo belongs to the field, not the game. The ↺ in the top chrome undoes the last game event; the one in the commit bar names the last thing you did on the diamond — Throw 3 · SS → C, or a runner drag, or a fielder tap — so a mis-tap on a crowded infield costs one press instead of a rebuild. It sits beside Commit play because that's where the thumb already is while the play is being built.
Runners read as runners. Runner tokens sit outboard of their bases so they never cover a fielder's tap target, and they carry the panel's wording (B, R2) — a bare "2" on the infield is unreadable next to a "2B" fielder and a second throw.
Named zones over false precision. "104 ft → 121 ft" implies a measurement nobody took. SHALLOW LEFT is what the scorer actually knows, and it aggregates into spray charts just as well.
The field is drawn to scale. One constant — 3.2 px per foot — sets every distance: 60 ft basepaths, a 43 ft mound, 200 ft down the lines and 220 ft to center, with the fence as a single 166.5 ft arc struck from a point 53.5 ft in front of the plate (the geometry that produces 200/220 exactly). The skinned infield follows the rulebook rather than a guess: a 60 ft radius struck from the center of the pitching circle — which is why the dirt wraps behind home plate — cut off 3 ft outside each foul line. Softball's fat infield means true scale still leaves the basepath diamond 271 px across, so nothing has to be faked to stay tappable. Bases and the plate are the one exception — at true scale they'd be 4 px, so they stay symbols.
Commit is where the thumb is. Full-width bottom bar with the plain-English result beside it, so the scorer reads what they're about to save. Undo and cancel stay top-right, away from it.
