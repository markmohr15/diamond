Notes on the ground view
Bands are named, not numbered. Your build marks depth with "1 ft" and "2 ft" tick labels that repeat above and below the plate — at a glance there's no telling a foot short from a foot past. Naming each band (SHORT 2 FT+, SHORT 1–2 FT, SHORT UNDER 1 FT, ON THE PLATE, PAST THE PLATE) makes the tap self-checking, and the callout on the ball prints the actual figure.
Box lines to scale, as drawn. Both inner batter's-box lines are real chalk width — 3 in, 36 px — with the inner edge 14.5 in from plate center, running the full length of the plane exactly as in your build. They are the user's lateral reference for how far off the plate a bounced ball landed, so they stay full-strength white, not decoration.
Depth reads as a wash. Short bands carry a dark tint and past-the-plate a light one, so front-to-back is legible in sun without leaning on the labels.
Same scale as the zone view. 12 px/in both ways: plate 17 in square, box lines 3 in wide with inner edges 14.5 in from center, a foot of depth 144 px. Switching views never rescales the field.
Nothing is orphaned. Count, outs, and undo stay up, so a ball in the dirt commits like any other pitch instead of dropping out of the sequence. The call code is already logged on the previous screen — repeating it here just competes with the one decision being made.
DEPTH UNKNOWN stays. Keeping it honest beats a guessed coordinate — it commits the pitch as in-the-dirt with no depth rather than forcing a tap.
