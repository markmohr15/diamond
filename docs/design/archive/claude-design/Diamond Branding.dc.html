Turn 03 · App icon and splash, light and dark
The mark holds at 40px because it’s two shapes, not a scene
The icon is the chalk baseline diamond with the clay home plate at its near vertex — the same two elements as the wordmark’s lockup, so the icon and the logo read as one thing. Light sits the chalk on Grass; dark sits it on Ink and lifts the chalk lines to Grass lit so the mark doesn’t glow harder than the app behind it. Plate stays Clay in both, and it’s the only saturated element at small size — that’s what makes the mark recognisable in a folder.

Splash is the same lockup at rest with the tagline set in mono, and a single hairline load bar. No animation, no gradient: the app opens in a dugout in daylight, so the light splash is Raised rather than pure white to survive glare, and the dark splash is Field so it doesn’t flash on a night game.

Turn 02 · After dark, and the three reserved colors
1A at night, and why amber survives on the chips but not in the HUD
The dark theme isn't an inversion — Ink stops being the darkest thing in the room and becomes the card, sitting on a deeper field below it. Grass and Clay both get lifted for use as line and text, because at their light-theme values they disappear against Ink. Nothing about the mark, the type, or the voice changes.

On the reserved colors: amber fails for uncertainty and works for misplay, and the reason is size. Chroma is what separates a colour from Clay, but large areas of high chroma are unreadable after two innings — so the big persistent element gets a low-chroma field with a saturated label, and the 40px chip gets a fully saturated fill. That split also answers the shared-colour question: they shouldn't share, because they ask the coach for different things.

Callable
NOT A SEMANTIC COLOUR
GRASS @ 14% · GRASS-LIT @ 18%
This one shouldn't get a hue of its own. The other three are events — something happened, look here. Callable is the opposite: a standing description of where the tool works, present on most zones most of the time. A dedicated colour would put a permanent alert-weight wash across half the grid and spend the palette's last unused hue on the least urgent thing in the app.

So: callable is Grass, and the collision is the point — wash, solid, dashed are three steps of one hue reading as availability, selection and exclusion. What it must never be is a fourth value called callableGreen that drifts a few degrees off Grass; that's the collision worth fearing, two greens neither of which looks intentional. Retire the token and point it at Grass with an opacity step.
The squint test
All five at chip size and adjacent, which is the worst case — a Clay accent sitting next to a Rosin chip on the same row. Amber and Clay only collide when both are mid-lightness and mid-chroma; separating them on both axes is what keeps this readable at arm's length in sun.
